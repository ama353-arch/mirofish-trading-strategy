# MiroFish Trading Strategy

**Agent swarm Monte Carlo simulation for trading strategy backtesting**

> Replace parameterized stochastic models with populations of heterogeneous AI agents — let emergent collective behavior generate market predictions.

Inspired by [MiroFish](https://github.com/666ghj/MiroFish), a swarm intelligence engine that constructs digital parallel worlds populated by thousands of AI agents with distinct personas, knowledge states, and behavioral logic.

---

## What This Is

Traditional quant strategies model market dynamics with stochastic processes — GBM, jump-diffusion, regime-switching. This system replaces the parametric distribution with an **agent-based generative model**:

1. **Build a knowledge graph** from news, social media, and market data (GraphRAG-inspired)
2. **Generate diverse agent personas** — retail bettors, institutional traders, political analysts, quants, contrarians — each with unique expertise, cognitive biases, and information diets
3. **Simulate social interaction** on a mock social platform where agents discuss, debate, and update beliefs over multiple rounds
4. **Extract probability distributions** from the swarm's collective final estimates
5. **Compare to market prices** and trade the divergence

The edge: swarm simulation produces narrative-aware, heterogeneous probability estimates that capture information traditional models structurally cannot — social dynamics, qualitative reasoning, reflexive feedback loops.

---

## Features

- **Agent Monte Carlo** — adaptive importance sampling where the proposal distribution is generated by agent interaction, not assumed parametrically
- **16 agent archetypes** with MBTI-derived cognitive profiles (risk tolerance, contrarian bias, decision speed, information processing style)
- **Dual market support** — prediction markets (Polymarket, Kalshi) and equity catalyst trading
- **Gelman-Rubin convergence diagnostics** — borrow from MCMC: only trade when R-hat < 1.1 across parallel simulations
- **Kelly criterion position sizing** — fractional Kelly with bankroll constraints (max 5% per position, 20% total deployed)
- **Five distinct strategy variants** — consensus divergence, sentiment momentum, contrarian swarm, event catalyst delta, cross-market arb
- **Backtesting framework** — historical simulation with transaction costs, slippage, drawdown tracking, Monte Carlo confidence intervals on equity curves
- **LLM-powered or rule-based** — run fast heuristic simulations with no API keys, or plug in GPT-4o-mini for richer agent reasoning
- **GraphRAG knowledge graph** — NetworkX-based entity-relationship graph seeded from news and social data

### Agent Monte Carlo — the mathematical core

Classical Monte Carlo samples from a parametric distribution P(X). Agent Monte Carlo replaces P(X) with a generative process:

```
P_swarm(X) = (1/N) * Σ δ(X - outcome(agent_i))
```

Each `agent_i` has:
- A **persona** θ_i (demographics, risk tolerance, expertise, cognitive biases)
- A **knowledge state** K_i(t) drawn from a shared knowledge graph plus private information
- A **behavioral policy** π_i(a | s, θ_i, K_i) governing actions (post, react, update beliefs)

This is formally equivalent to **adaptive importance sampling** — the swarm generates a proposal distribution Q that approximates the true market distribution P better than market-implied prices M when:

```
D_KL(P || Q) < D_KL(P || M)
```

### Convergence diagnostics

Borrowed from MCMC: run M parallel simulations and compute the Gelman-Rubin R-hat:

```
R-hat = sqrt(Var_between / Var_within)
```

Trade only when R-hat < 1.1 (simulations have converged) and effective sample size N_eff > 100.

## Five Strategy Variants

All five strategies use the agent swarm simulation as the underlying engine, but differ in what signal they extract and how they size positions.

### Strategy 1 — Consensus Divergence

The baseline approach. Run a standard swarm simulation, extract the mean swarm probability, and trade when it diverges from the market price by more than 5 percentage points. Requires convergence (R-hat < 1.1). Position sized via fractional Kelly (25% of full Kelly).

**Signal**: `alpha = p_swarm - p_market`

**Trade when**: `|alpha| > 0.05` and `swarm_std < 0.15` and `converged`

### Strategy 2 — Sentiment Momentum

Trades the *trajectory* of agent sentiment across simulation rounds, not the final probability level. Fits a linear trend to aggregate sentiment evolution and computes momentum (slope) and acceleration. A swarm shifting from bearish to bullish is a stronger signal than one that starts and stays bullish.

**Signal**: `momentum = d(S_bull)/d(round)` via linear regression on sentiment trajectory

### Strategy 3 — Contrarian Swarm

Overweights contrarian agent personas (30-40% of population vs. the standard 10%) and isolates their probability estimates. Trades when contrarians strongly disagree with both the market price and the majority consensus — designed to capture mean-reversion after narrative-driven overshooting.

### Strategy 4 — Event Catalyst Delta

Runs two simulations per event: pre-event (background context only) and post-event (with new information injected). Trades the delta between pre and post estimates when the market hasn't fully adjusted — capturing the information processing lag across diverse participant types.

**Signal**: `delta = p_post - p_pre`

### Strategy 5 — Cross-Market Arbitrage

Compares the full probability distribution from the swarm (not just the mean) against both prediction market prices and equity options implied probabilities. Extracts tail probabilities and checks for divergence against options-implied move distributions — exploiting the fact that prediction markets and options markets aggregate systematically different participant populations.

## Two Modes of Operation

### Rule-based (default — no API keys required)

- Agents use heuristic reasoning based on persona traits (MBTI, risk tolerance, contrarian bias)
- Social influence modeled as weighted belief averaging with contrarian inversion
- Fast: 100 agents × 30 rounds × 10 simulations runs in seconds
- Good for: backtesting, rapid prototyping, parameter sweeps

### LLM-powered (requires OpenAI API key)

- Each agent reasons through its persona via structured LLM prompts
- Produces rich, narratively coherent opinions and cross-agent influence
- Estimated cost: ~$2-5 per event with GPT-4o-mini
- Good for: live signal generation, high-stakes events, research

The rule-based mode captures the essential swarm dynamics — heterogeneous beliefs, social influence, contrarian behavior, convergence — well enough for backtesting and most research purposes.
