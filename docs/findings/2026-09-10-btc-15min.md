# Kalshi 15-Minute BTC: Findings

## Question
The edge map rejected hourly BTC. KXBTC15M has a structural feature the hourly strike market does not: both
ends are 60-second averages of CF BRTI, and the closing average accumulates one price per second in the final
minute. Does that — or anything else in this market — give an edge after Kalshi fees?

## The contract (verified from the Kalshi API, 2026-09-10)
- "BTC price up in next 15 mins?" — Yes if the simple average of the 60 BRTI prices before close is at least the
  average of the 60 before open. The opening average is published as the strike at open; the next window's strike
  equals this window's settlement value.
- 96 windows a day. Median **1.72M contracts per window** (~$1.7M), rising from 1.26M (early July) to 2.01M
  (September). One of the most liquid products on Kalshi — the opposite of the edge map's thin-market hypothesis.
- Base rate: 3,153 yes / 3,165 no. A coin flip.
- Price grid: 0.1¢ steps below 10¢ and above 90¢; 1¢ steps in between.
- Fees (Kalshi help centre + API docs): taker 0.07·C·P·(1−P), maker 0.0175·C·P·(1−P), multiplier 1 for this
  series, rounded to a hundredth of a cent. Which markets charge maker fees is not published. Approved market
  makers get "reduced fees". The official fee PDF blocks automated fetches (HTTP 429) and was not read.
- API gotcha: the legacy `volume` / `open_interest` fields read 0 on these markets. Real values are in `volume_fp`
  / `open_interest_fp`.

## Method
- Executable prices from real trades: ask ≈ last YES-taker trade price, bid ≈ last NO-taker trade price, at most
  5s stale, observed 1s after the decision. Mid-window checkpoints use Kalshi's 1-minute bid/ask candles.
- Discovery = first half of windows by date, Confirmation = second half. A finding counts only if it shows up in both.
- Bets on heavy favourites are judged on **loss counts** (exact Poisson test against the break-even number of
  losses), never on t-stats. With near-zero losses a t-stat reports certainty the sample does not have: the first
  pass showed t = +19 to +28 on 97–99¢ favourites; the exact test showed nothing.
- Windows are the independent unit for every standard error.
- BRTI stand-in: Binance BTCUSDT 1-second closes, checked against Kalshi's published strike and settlement for
  every window before use.
- About 700 configurations were tried across all tests. Nothing below should be read without that number in mind.

## Results

| Thesis | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Terminal-minute settlement math | **Dead (free data)** | Market beats the model's Brier score at all 22 checkpoints (confirm half); gap widest at the end (10s before close: market 0.0043, model 0.0604). Model trade rule loses on discovery at all 24 settings. |
| Open displacement | **Dead** | Model worse than market in the first minute; trading it loses 2.3–3.0¢/contract in both halves. |
| Market calibration | **Honest** | Calibration slopes 0.77–1.22 through the window (1.0 = prices are true probabilities). No mid-price cell survived discovery → confirmation. |
| Favourites 90–99.9¢, last 5 min → 30s | **Dead** | Pooled, one trade per window, all 6,318 windows: every band flat or negative, all p ≥ 0.53. |
| Favourites ≥99¢, last 2–15s | **Unproven lead** | 1,612 trades, 0 losses vs 3.9 break-even, +0.24¢/contract. Exact p = 0.21 (discovery), 0.09 (confirm), 0.02 (all) — chosen after seeing the data, out of ~700 tries. |
| Average maker | **Small, real** | Takers pay resting orders +0.6–0.8¢ gross in the first 120s at mid prices (+0.2–0.4¢ after maker fee), +0.22¢ gross in last-120s tails; significant in both halves. |
| Naive maker (us) | **Dead** | Loses in every setup, both halves. First 120s: −0.36¢/fill even at the front of the queue, −1.6¢ when filled only on trade-through. Re-quoting every 2s: unchanged (−0.37¢). |

**Proxy check.** Binance vs BRTI, change over the window: mean error $3.20 (discovery) / $3.97 (confirm),
95th percentile $7.90 / $10.20; the proxy calls the outcome correctly on 98.75% / 97.63% of windows. Median
|settle − strike| is $50, but 6.4% of windows finish within $5 — exactly where the proxy's error decides the
answer. The market (which sees real BRTI) is far better informed than the proxy in the final minute.

**Final-seconds lead, economics.** Average price 99.74¢, so one loss wipes out ~410 wins. Fillable size after
the signal (favourite-side taker volume at or below our price in the next 10s): median 172 contracts, 25th
percentile zero — about $0.41 profit per trade at the median. Acting requires a bot firing in the last 15
seconds, which SCOPE.md's human-tap execution boundary forbids, in a race against faster bots.

## Conclusion
- **No tradeable strategy for this market at our tier** (free data, human speed or second-level automation).
  Takers face an efficient, honestly-calibrated price. The profit that makers earn goes to fast professionals,
  who may also pay reduced fees; a naive maker is the one being picked off.
- This confirms the edge map rather than breaking it: the most liquid, highest-attention product on the venue is
  efficient.
- The final-seconds favourite lead is not worth building: unproven, tiny per trade, catastrophic single loss,
  needs a scope change. If kept alive at all, the honest next step is a **read-only forward log** of the last 15
  seconds for ~2 months to see whether losses stay under break-even — no money.
- Cross-venue with Polymarket's matching 15-minute market was **not tested**: it settles on a different source
  (Chainlink BTC/USD 60s TWAP), trades roughly 100× less (~$13.5k on one window checked), and third-party reports
  say Polymarket charges taker fees of up to ~1.8% on exactly these markets to stop speed arbitrage. Not an arb.
- Recommended next build is unchanged from the edge map: the **liquidity-stratified calibration scan** across all
  Kalshi categories.

## Reproducing
The fetcher and analyses were throwaway spike scripts in the session scratchpad and are not in the repo. A full
re-run needs a fresh fetch (~45 min for the Kalshi tape at ~2.4 windows/s, ~330MB of Binance files).
