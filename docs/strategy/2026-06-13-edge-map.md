# Where Prediction-Market Edge Actually Exists

This is the synthesis of everything tested. The goal: stop hunting edge by trial
and error, and target deliberately. The one-line thesis we arrived at:

> **Edge tracks inefficiency. Inefficiency tracks LOW liquidity, LOW attention,
> structural friction, and execution advantage — NOT prediction skill in popular
> markets.** Every liquid, popular market we tested was efficient. The engine
> works; we were fishing where everyone fishes.

---

## What we built (the real asset)

A disciplined research engine, ~131 tests, that **refuses to report a fake edge**:

- Walk-forward OOS harness: no look-ahead, spread-crossing costs, **deflated
  Sharpe** (multiple-testing correction), block-bootstrap MC.
- Calibration harness: Brier, log-loss, reliability, Platt scaling.
- Fair-value pricer (lognormal `N(d2)`); sports win-prob (NBA diffusion, soccer
  Poisson); momentum (fade/follow); arbitrage detector; portfolio (frac-Kelly +
  caps + correlation); ensemble.
- Unified resolved-event schema + READ-ONLY fetchers (Kalshi, Polymarket Gamma,
  ESPN play-by-play, Coinbase spot, Deribit IV).

**The engine is the moat.** It caught every fake edge this session produced:
+7.3% synthetic-data alpha, +347% settlement bug, +44%/+28% non-partition
"arbs," +20% small-sample BTC. None survived scrutiny. That discipline is what
lets capital be deployed without being lost.

## The map: where edge lives vs doesn't

**Does NOT live here (proven efficient — stop spending time):**
- Predictive edges in high-liquidity, high-attention markets (NBA Finals ~$70M/
  game, hourly BTC, major events).
- Internal structural arb on a single liquid venue.
- Swarm-mechanics tuning without an information source.

**Most likely lives here (untested or unrefuted, ranked by expected value):**
1. **Low-liquidity / low-attention markets.** Thin books, obscure events, where
   retail mispricing and stale prices persist. The deliberate test: a
   liquidity-stratified calibration scan — bucket markets by volume, measure
   Brier + favorite-longshot bias per bucket. Edge = where the market is poorly
   calibrated. *Highest EV, directly tests the core thesis.*
2. **Cross-venue arb (Kalshi vs Polymarket).** Two venues can genuinely disagree
   on the same event. Market-neutral, no prediction. Cost: event-matching across
   venues is messy.
3. **Maker execution / be-the-liquidity.** The BTC signal was +EV at mid; the
   spread was the killer. Posting (not crossing) flips taker losses into the
   spread you *earn* — but requires real-time infra and honest adverse-selection
   modeling ("you get filled when you're wrong").
4. **Settlement / structural quirks.** Specific contract mechanics (Kalshi BRTI
   60-sec smoothing, Polymarket Chainlink oracle latency). Niche, fragile.
5. **A genuinely better-than-market model or faster/proprietary info.** Hard —
   the market out-predicted ESPN-grade models. Needs a real data advantage.

## Methodology rules (earned this session)

1. **Any riskless return > spread, or any per-trade return > ~spread on a binary,
   is a bug until proven otherwise.** Magnitude is the first tell.
2. **Calibration ≠ profit.** Being more accurate doesn't beat the spread.
3. **In-sample is noise.** Always OOS + deflated Sharpe; small samples lie
   (BTC flipped sign from 60 to 142 markets).
4. **Settle correctly.** A sell = buy NO at `1-bid`; mis-settlement created the
   +347% mirage.
5. **Verify the structure.** "Arb" math only holds on true mutually-exclusive +
   exhaustive partitions — not parlays or nested above/below strikes.

## Recommended next build (deliberate)

**Liquidity-stratified calibration scan across all Kalshi (+ Polymarket)
categories.** Pull resolved markets with a pre-resolution price, bucket by
volume, compute calibration per bucket. Hypothesis: the lowest-volume buckets are
systematically miscalibrated (a tradeable, prediction-free bias). This targets
edge by its most reliable proxy — *inefficiency* — instead of by arena. If even
the thinnest markets are calibrated, the honest conclusion is that retail
prediction markets are broadly efficient and the only edge is maker execution /
infrastructure, which is a different (engineering) game.
